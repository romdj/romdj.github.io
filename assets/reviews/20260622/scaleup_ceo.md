# Scale-up CEO Review — 2026-06-22

> **Scope:** exec-summary one-pager (`/`) only.
> **Resume state at review time:** title *"Engineering Leader: Architecture, Direction-setting, Operating Model for AI-native engineering"*, tagline dropped, 6 Selected Impact bullets (IAM track ownership, group-wide governance, CCP vision, Nike OTE, Philips HSDP, Z8 multi-year agentic AI practitioner).

### 1. Snap verdict
Reads as a group-level enterprise architect with an AI-native vocabulary bolted on top. Fit score **2/5**.

### 2. Would I take the call?
**No.** The one-pager sells governance authorship, not shipped product or team ownership, and I am hiring someone who runs an engineering org, not someone who defends visions at an Architecture Review Board.

### 3. What works
- *"Architecture lead for the most senior team at Nike (12 people, Outbound Transportation Execution)... Deconstructed a 15-year-old monolith and migrated to Centiro then Blue Yonder as next-gen TMS. Daily delivery with on-call accountability."* This is the only line that smells like delivery and accountability at scale-up tempo. It is the spine of any call I would take.
- *"Most engineering leaders are either deep technologists who have never run an AI-native org, or AI-hype operators with no governance spine. I bring both. AI-augmented engineering is my daily driver, not my reading list."* Good positioning sentence. Maps to a real gap in the market.
- *"Multi-year practitioner of agentic AI in engineering work... Most recent applied artifact: /resume-review, a multi-agent system with isolated reviewer subagents, anti-flattery enforcement, and sycophancy audit."* Operator signal — tells me they actually build with these tools, not just talk about them.
- *"Backend platform contributor at Philips HealthSuite Digital Platform... supporting 1B+ device connections per year. FHIR and HIPAA compliant."* Medtech credibility is concrete and regulatory-aware.
- "Sector Experience" table cleanly maps Medtech / Energy / Sports tech to specific companies. Earns its space in a 60-second scan.

### 4. What does not work
- *"I lead engineering direction at group level, setting the technical principles and governance that steer organizations toward shared business outcomes."* This is the opening sentence and it is pure architect. It does not say "I run engineering teams." It says "I write principles." I read that and I move on.
- *"ARB-ratified visions across 300+ regulated participants,"* *"Vision ratified at the Architecture Review Board,"* *"Defended at the Architecture Review Board to Business, CIO, CISO, and Group Architecture stakeholders."* Three bullets in a row whose verb is *defend a vision at a committee*. In a scale-up that is not a credential, it is a warning sign about stakeholder soup and slow tempo.
- *"Established group-wide governance practice at Belgium's national TSO. API and messaging standards across ~100 engineering teams, ~50 applications, and ~70 data products, plus organizational fitness functions defining minimum architecture-quality thresholds."* Zero of those 100 teams reported to the candidate. Authoring standards across teams you do not own is not the same as running an engineering org and the resume does not distinguish them.
- The Selected Impact list contains six bullets and the only one mentioning shipped product with delivery accountability is Nike, which is the oldest. Philips is "contributor." Elia is three layers of vision and governance. The center of gravity of this one-pager is 2024-2026 work that is documents, not shipped systems.
- *"Leadership & direction"* capability is described as *"Architecture Review Board defense and presentation. Direct alignment with CIO / CTO levels and business leadership (Director, VP, EVP)."* That is the description of an enterprise architect's stakeholder map, not an engineering leader's job. Nothing about hiring, firing, performance management, on-call rotation, budget ownership, or quarterly delivery. Those are the things I am buying.

### 5. Toughest interview questions I would ask
1. You have never carried a P&L or had a single person report to you on paper. Walk me through the hardest performance conversation you have ever had with someone who was not your direct report, and what changed in their behaviour after.
2. Pick one of the ARB-ratified visions. It is now 18 months later. What percent of it actually shipped, what is stuck, and who is the engineer in that 100-team estate who will tell me the truth about why.
3. At Nike you led 12 people. Were any of them hired by you, fired by you, or promoted by you. If not, who did, and what does that tell me about whether you can run a team of 15 in my scale-up next quarter.
4. You position AI-native operating model as your wedge. Show me one production system, in someone else's hands, where your verification-first pattern is running today and producing measurable change. Not a CLAUDE.md file. A system.
5. My runway is 14 months. In your first 90 days here, what specifically would you stop doing that you do today at Elia, and what is the first thing you would ship by week 6.

### 6. One thing I would change in the resume
The Profile section opens with the wrong verb.

**Before:** *"I lead engineering direction at group level, setting the technical principles and governance that steer organizations toward shared business outcomes, bridging enterprise-grade rigor with AI-native practice."*

**After:** *"I run engineering teams that ship in regulated environments. At Nike I led a 12-person team that replaced a 15-year-old monolith powering multi-million daily events without missing a delivery window. At Elia I set the group-wide governance spine that lets 100 teams move without diverging. Now I want to do both in one seat, in a scale-up."*

The current opening sells the architect. The replacement sells the leader who happens to bring architecture rigor with him. That reorders what I read in the first six seconds.

### 7. Final recommendation
**Pass.**
