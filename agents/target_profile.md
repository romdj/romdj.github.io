# Target Profile

> What the candidate is targeting. Used by the reviewer agents
> (`scaleup_ceo.md`, `beangels_investor.md`, `takingturns_recruiter.md`)
> as the anchor against which to evaluate the resume. Honest by design:
> includes the gaps that need to be bridged, not only the ambition.

## Role

**Scale-up technical leadership: Head of Engineering or CTO** at a
~30 to 300-person company. Strategy-led and both-worlds (business +
technical). Not a pure people-manager VP-Eng seat. Not a theoretical
Enterprise / Chief Architect seat.

The candidate is moving from a Solution Architect track (most recent:
group-level architecture at Belgium's national TSO) into the leadership
seat where architectural authority becomes formal management.

## Sectors (run in parallel; let the network decide which warms up)

- **Medtech.** Fastest network access through warm intros; real prior
  experience (Philips HSDP, Extra Horizon, FibriCheck).
- **Sports tech.** Highest personal conviction; deepest authentic
  credibility (Nike, lifelong multisport athlete, community organizer).
- **Energy tech.** Strongest pure-domain logic; currently at the national
  TSO. Network built from scratch.

Deep tech is a modifier on any of the above, not a separate lane. The
candidate has no strong opinion either way on deep-tech-flavoured
opportunities.

## Employment structure

In Belgium, above approximately 100k/year, many leaders opt for the
management-company freelance route for tax optimization. On that basis,
the candidate will lean toward freelance contracts.

This is a compensation-structure preference, not a fundamental one.
Reviewers should not read it as "the candidate avoids commitment". If
freelance versus permanent is a make-or-break for the role, the
candidate is flexible either way.

Equity component welcome, not required.

## Positioning thesis

A pragmatic, delivery-first technical leader bridging enterprise-grade
architecture rigor and scale-up execution speed. Operates at
direction-setting altitude, stays close to the actual business, leads by
initiative and competence rather than by title.

## What "right fit" looks like

- High-autonomy, high-variety, strategy and 0→1 / 1→N roles.
- Founder or CEO comfortable sharing technical direction.
- Scope to pair the candidate's direction-setting strength with either
  (a) a strong delivery #2, or (b) a stage where the people load is
  small and learnable.
- Selling into enterprise / regulated environments is a strength
  multiplier.

## What "wrong fit" looks like

- Pure relentless operational grind with no support structure.
- Pure people-manager VP-Eng seat. This is not the candidate's preferred
  lane, and they are honest about not having that muscle yet.
- Theoretical Enterprise / Chief Architect seat with no execution.
- Another individual-contributor Solution Architect role: a sideways
  step.
- Big-bureaucracy environment.
- US-based roles (geography exclusion).

## Honest gaps to evaluate against

A sharp hiring manager will probe these. Reviewers should not give the
candidate a pass on them.

1. **No prior engineering-management title.** Architectural authority
   over 20+ teams; led delivery and quality initiatives without line
   authority. Bridge framing the candidate uses: seeking the seat where
   that becomes formal, with the people-management craft built
   deliberately.

2. **Enterprise background, scale-up chaos concern.** Bridge: builder by
   default outside work (own services company, property portfolio,
   product venture). Enterprise rigor framed as an asset for scale-ups
   selling into enterprise, not a sign of slowness.

3. **People-management muscle untested at the "own the team / hard
   conversations" level.** Bridge: do not oversell. Seek roles where
   direction-setting strength can be paired with a strong delivery #2,
   or where the people load is small and learnable.

The candidate is iterating on the resume positioning. Not yet active in

## How reviewers should read the resume against this target

- Evaluate fit against the role, sector, stage, and geography above.
- Be honest about whether the resume actually sells leadership, or
  whether it still reads as a senior IC / architect track.
- Name specific bullets, words, or sections that work or do not.
- Do not flatter. If you would not recommend a call, say so plainly.
- Use specific, quotable feedback the candidate can act on.
