### 5. What does not work
- This is an enterprise-architect CV with an AI sticker. The verbs are *"Authored," "Established," "Anchored," "Defended at the Architecture Review Board."* Nothing says shipped, sold, hired, fired, retained, grew revenue, cut burn. A scaleup CTO does the latter.
- *"Architecture lead for the most senior team at Nike (12 people, Outbound Transportation Execution)"* — architecture lead, not engineering manager. The target says scaleup CTO/Head of Engineering. This page does not show a P&L, a hiring scorecard, a comp band owned, an attrition number, or one named direct report. The gap is undisguised.
- The AI bullet underdelivers. *"15+ CLAUDE.md configurations"* and a `/resume-review` slash command is a hobbyist's signal, not an operator's. For someone whose pitch is AI-native operating model, I expect a shipped product, a team running this in production, or a paying customer — none here.
- Enterprise scale signals (*"300+ market participants," "1B+ device connections per year," "~100 engineering teams"*) read as TSO/Philips/Nike scale, not scaleup reality. I worry about a culture shock at 30–80 person companies and the resume does nothing to pre-empt that worry.
- Zero commercial fluency on the page. Not one sentence about unit economics, customer acquisition, contract value, runway, or hiring cost. For a CTO seat near a founder, that is the conversation that gets had on day one.
- The Selected Impact list is six bullets and five of them are architecture artifacts (visions, standards, fitness functions, ratifications). One person, one register. I'd want at least one bullet that's about people or money.

- A live walk through one unit-economics or burn-rate conversation they have actually had — pick a real number from a real company.
- Concrete evidence of people-management craft: a hire they made, a person they let go, an attrition number they own. The target profile flags this gap; I need to hear them not flinch on it.
- One AI-native operating-model artifact deployed inside a team they did not personally own — i.e., somebody else picked it up and used it. Otherwise the AI pillar is a personal-productivity hobby.
- Why scaleup and why now, with a coherent answer to "given Elia is paying you well, what's the trigger to leave for a 50-person company at risk-adjusted comp?"
