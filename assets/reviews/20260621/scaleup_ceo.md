# Scale-up CEO Review — 2026-06-21

> **Scope:** exec-summary one-pager (`/`) only. Detail page (`/detail/`) was out of scope for this pass.
> **Resume state at review time:** title was *"Cross-functional Leader: Engineering, Architecture, Direction"*, tagline *"I make small, AI-augmented teams safe to run fast"*, 5 Selected Impact bullets pre-rewrite, certifications still on one-pager, birthdate + driver's license still on contact.

### 1. Snap verdict
Architect-shaped one-pager with a CTO tagline glued on top. Fit score **2/5**.

### 2. Would I take the call?
**No.** The page sells governance authorship, not a leader who has owned outcomes, headcount, or P&L pressure — and the only leadership artifact ("Architecture lead for a 12-person team at Nike") is a track-lead framing, not a management one.

### 3. What works
- The tagline is crisp and points at the right problem: *"I make small, AI-augmented teams safe to run fast."* That is a real thesis a CEO can repeat to a board.
- The wedge paragraph names the trade-off directly: *"Most engineering leaders are either deep technologists who have never run an AI-native org, or AI-hype operators with no governance spine. I bring both."* That is the only line on the page that actually positions for a CTO seat.
- Concrete scale numbers are present and varied: *"1B+ device connections per year"*, *"multi-million daily events at peak"*, *"300+ market participants"*. These survive a 60-second skim.
- Sector triangulation is legible at a glance via the Sector Experience table — Medtech, Energy, Sports tech each anchored to a recognizable name (Philips, Elia, Nike). That is the right format for a pre-read.
- Regulatory literacy is named in usable shorthand: *"NIS2 / DORA / eIDAS 2.0"* and *"IEC 62304, FHIR"*. For a CEO selling into enterprise/regulated buyers, that is a strength multiplier.

### 4. What does not work
- The title is incoherent for a CTO/Head-of-Eng pitch: *"Cross-functional Leader: Engineering, Architecture, Direction"*. "Cross-functional Leader" is what someone writes when they are not yet a leader of anything specific. A CEO scanning this thinks "consultant" or "architect," not "owner."
- Every "leadership" bullet is architecture authorship, not delivery ownership. *"Authored the DIF / IAM Architecture Vision"*, *"Established group-wide API and messaging standards"*, *"delivering 3 intermediate solution designs"*. This is a Chief Architect resume — exactly the "theoretical Enterprise / Chief Architect seat" the target profile says is the wrong fit.
- No team owned, no hiring, no firing, no budget, no headcount growth, no attrition figure, no business-outcome metric. *"Architecture lead for a 12-person team at Nike"* is the closest thing — and "Architecture lead" is not "led." A CEO reading for line management sees zero evidence.
- The AI-native claim is asserted, not demonstrated: *"Hands-on agentic AI as a daily collaborator"*. No shipped AI-augmented team, no verification-pipeline they built, no eval framework, no agentic workflow they instituted. For someone whose strategic POV is that "the engineering bottleneck has moved from production to verification," the page carries none of that thesis. The wedge fails to land.
- *"Unblocked 2 development teams from a quarter-long delay"* by delivering *"3 intermediate solution designs"* — this is a senior IC unblocking peers, not a leader running a system. Putting it in the Selected Impact list of a CTO one-pager actively undermines the seat the candidate is targeting.
- Capability bloat at the bottom dilutes the pitch: *"Cloud (AWS, Azure, GCP, self-hosted OpenShift). Software (Node.js / TypeScript, Python, Scala / Java, .NET)."* A CEO does not hire a CTO on a stack list.
- Personal-data noise on a senior leadership pre-read: *"25/06/1991"*, *"Driver's License: B"*. Junior-CV reflex; signals lack of taste about the artifact's audience.

### 5. Toughest interview questions I would ask
1. You say *"I make small, AI-augmented teams safe to run fast."* Name a team you ran — not advised, not aligned with, ran — and tell me the velocity-versus-divergence trade-off you actually made. What did you give up?
2. You have authored standards across *"20+ teams"* and *"~100 engineering teams"* but I don't see a single bullet where you owned headcount or hired anyone. Walk me through the last person you fired and the last person you hired. If neither exists, tell me why you think you're ready for this seat.
3. Your one-pager's leading impact bullet is *"Authored the DIF / IAM Architecture Vision … ratified at the Architecture Review Board."* When the multi-year roadmap slips by two quarters next year, who in your org owns that, and what did you change in the operating model to catch it earlier?
4. The wedge says *"AI-augmented engineering is my daily driver, not my reading list."* Show me the verification pipeline, the eval harness, or the guardrail you have personally put into production. Be specific about what broke and what you changed.
5. The Nike line says *"Architecture lead for a 12-person team."* Were you the manager of those 12 people, the technical authority over them, or a peer the team leaned on? If it's the second or third, what is your honest plan to acquire the first muscle in the next 6 months — on my payroll?

### 6. One thing I would change in the resume
The title. The current line frames the candidate as an advisor.

**Before:** *"Cross-functional Leader: Engineering, Architecture, Direction"*

**After:** *"Head of Engineering / CTO — governance spine for AI-native delivery"*

The title is the second thing on the page. Right now it is the line that closes the door. The replacement names the seat being sought and the differentiator in one breath.

### 7. Final recommendation
**Pass.**
