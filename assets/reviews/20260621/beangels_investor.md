# BeAngels Investor Review — 2026-06-21

> **Scope:** exec-summary one-pager (`/`) only. Detail page (`/detail/`) was out of scope for this pass.
> **Resume state at review time:** title was *"Cross-functional Leader: Engineering, Architecture, Direction"*, tagline *"I make small, AI-augmented teams safe to run fast"*, 5 Selected Impact bullets pre-rewrite.

### 1. Snap verdict
Architect with an AI-native tagline pasted on top. Bet score **2/5**.

### 2. Operator signal
**Absent.** Nothing on this one-pager points to an own venture, side company, property portfolio, customer revenue, or P&L ownership. The closest the page comes is *"lead through initiative and competence rather than positional authority"* — that is employee framing, not operator framing. No GitHub repo highlighted beyond the handle `romdj`. No product shipped under the candidate's own name. No "I founded / I built / I sold / I run". The persona's own bias test ("side companies, real estate, builds") returns zero hits on this page.

### 3. Would I invest in a venture they were starting?
**No.** I cannot see a venture, a thesis with skin in the game, or evidence the candidate has ever carried commercial risk. This one-pager is a senior employee's CV, not a founder's pitch.

### 4. What works
- Geography and language stack are clean for Belgian portfolio work: *"Uccle (Brussels), Belgium"* plus French/English native, Dutch B1.2. That is exactly the BeNeLux profile I can actually deploy.
- Regulated-environment vocabulary is concrete and current: *"anchored in NIS2 / DORA / eIDAS 2.0"* plus *"IEC 62304, FHIR"*. Most candidates wave at compliance — this one names the regimes. Useful for portfolio companies selling into banks, health systems, or TSOs.
- One quantified, board-level artifact: *"Vision ratified at the Architecture Review Board with C-level visibility; multi-year roadmap currently in delivery."* That is the only line that suggests the candidate has had to defend a decision to people who could kill it.
- Sector triangulation matches the target profile cleanly: Medtech (Philips, Extra Horizon, FibriCheck), Energy (Elia, Engie), Sports tech (Nike). Three warm-intro lanes is unusually good optionality.
- The tagline *"I make small, AI-augmented teams safe to run fast."* is the only sentence on the page that sounds like a point of view rather than a job description. It is the right wedge for 2026.

### 5. What does not work
- The headline is a category error. *"Cross-functional Leader: Engineering, Architecture, Direction"* reads as a hedge across three identities. An angel wants one. Pick: are you a CTO, a Head of Engineering, or a Chief Architect? "Direction" is not a role.
- The AI-native thesis is asserted, never demonstrated. *"AI-augmented engineering is my daily driver, not my reading list"* — fine, prove it. The Capabilities line *"Hands-on agentic AI as a daily collaborator"* is a sentence, not evidence. No agent shipped, no workflow described, no metric. This is exactly the "name-drop frameworks they have read about" failure mode my persona warns against.
- Zero P&L, zero revenue, zero customer count owned. The biggest numbers — *"1B+ device connections per year"*, *"multi-million daily events at peak"*, *"300+ market participants"* — are scale of the employer's system, not value the candidate created or captured. I cannot tell a portfolio CEO what this person moved.
- No people-management evidence on a page positioning for a leadership seat. *"Architecture lead for a 12-person team at Nike"* is the only team-size number, and "lead" here is architectural, not managerial. The target profile flags "No prior engineering-management title" as a gap; the one-pager confirms the gap without bridging it.
- Certifications section actively damages the pitch. *"AWS Certified Developer - Associate"*, *"Agile SCRUM Foundation"*, *"FHIR Compliance"* from Udemy — these are journeyman badges, not CTO signal. My persona discounts certs as "what someone does instead of shipping". Six of them on a one-pager is loud.
- *"Unblocked 2 development teams from a quarter-long delay... Initiative taken without formal authority, in support of colleagues driving the target-state architecture."* Translated: someone else owned it, this candidate helped. That is a peer line, not a leader line, and it is on the Selected Impact list.

### 6. What I would want to see in a 30-minute conversation
- Show me your agent stack live. Open Claude Code, walk me through one real workflow you ran this week, including the verification gate you designed so the agent did not mark its own homework.
- Name one P&L number you have moved. Revenue, cost, time-to-market, churn — pick one and own it. If the answer is "I am an architect, I do not have one", explain how you will get one in a CTO seat.
- The hard people conversation. Tell me about the last time you fired someone, performance-managed someone out, or told a senior engineer their work was not good enough. If you have not, say so plainly and tell me how you will close that gap in the first 90 days.
- The DIF / IAM Vision at Elia — who tried to kill it, why, and how you kept it alive. I want the political knife-fight version, not the architecture diagram.
- The operator question. What have you built outside your employer in the last three years — a company, a product with paying users, a property, anything where you carried the downside? If the answer is nothing, why not?

### 7. One thing I would change in the resume
The headline. It is the first thing scanned and it currently splits the bet three ways.

**Before:** *"Cross-functional Leader: Engineering, Architecture, Direction"*

**After:** *"Head of Engineering for AI-native scale-ups in regulated markets"*

That commits to a seat (Head of Engineering), a stage (scale-up), a wedge (AI-native), and a moat (regulated). An angel can route that profile in 10 seconds. The current one forces me to guess.

### 8. Final recommendation
**Worth a coffee.** The regulated-markets vocabulary, the three-sector triangulation, and the Brussels base are real assets, and the AI-native wedge is correctly identified. But this one-pager sells an architect with leadership aspirations, not a leader. Until I see operator evidence or a P&L number, I am not pushing this to a portfolio CEO as a CTO candidate. Coffee, probe the gaps in section 6, then decide.
