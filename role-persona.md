## Target

**Role:** Scale-up technical leadership — **Head of Engineering / CTO** at a ~30–100 person company.
Strategy-led and both-worlds (business + technical). *Not* a pure people-manager VP-Eng seat; *not* a theoretical Enterprise/Chief Architect seat.

**Sectors (run in parallel, let the network decide which warms up):**
- **Medtech** — fastest network access (warm intros), real prior experience.
- **Sports tech** — highest personal conviction; deepest authentic credibility.
- **Energy tech** — strongest pure-domain logic; network built from scratch.
- *Deep tech* — a modifier on any of the above, not a separate lane.

## Positioning thesis (one line)

A pragmatic, delivery-first technical leader who bridges enterprise-grade architecture rigor and scale-up execution speed — operates at direction-setting altitude, stays close to the actual business, and leads by initiative and competence rather than by title.

## Part B — What target entities expect from me

### Generic scale-up Head of Eng / CTO expectations
A founder/board hiring at this level expects evidence of:
- **Owned delivery** — a clear outcome you were accountable for end-to-end, not just designed.
- **Team building** — ability to hire, grow, and (when needed) manage out; set up the engineering org and its cadence.
- **Technical direction** — make the architecture/build-vs-buy calls and defend them.
- **Hands-on-enough** — willing to get into the work; no ivory tower.
- **Ambiguity tolerance** — operate without enterprise scaffolding, resourcing, or process.
- **Commercial awareness** — connect engineering to runway, GTM, and unit economics.
- **AI-era fluency** — see the "AI-era technical-leader layer" below.

### Sector-specific expectations
- **Medtech:** regulatory/quality awareness (ISO 13485, IEC 62304, MDR-adjacent), clinical-stakeholder fluency, data privacy. Slower, evidence-driven cycles.
- **Sports tech:** consumer/product sensibility, brand instinct, hardware + software literacy, community/athlete authenticity.
- **Energy tech:** grid/utility domain, regulated-environment patterns, tolerance for longer (sometimes hardware) cycles, security/identity at infrastructure scale.

## Part C — Gaps to preempt (and how to bridge them honestly)

These are the objections a sharp hiring manager will raise. Address proactively in the narrative.

1. **No prior engineering-management title.**
   - *Bridge:* I've held architectural authority over 20+ teams and led delivery and quality initiatives without line authority — the leverage was competence and initiative. I'm seeking the seat where that becomes formal, and I'm clear-eyed that the people-management craft is the part I'll build deliberately, not pretend I've already mastered.

2. **Enterprise background → scale-up chaos concern.**
   - *Bridge:* I'm a builder by default outside work (own company, property, product venture) — comfortable with ambiguity and doing it myself. The enterprise rigor is an *asset* for a scale-up selling into enterprises, not a sign I can't move fast.

3. **People-management muscle is real but untested at the "own the team / hard conversations" level.**
   - *Bridge:* Don't oversell it. Seek roles where I can pair direction-setting strength with either a strong delivery #2 or a stage where the people load is small and learnable. Be honest that I find the hard parts a worthwhile price, not a thrill — and that's fine for the right structure.

> **Role-design note (for me, not the resume):** I do my best work in high-autonomy, high-variety, strategy-and-0→1 roles, and I perform best with external accountability and operational scaffolding around sustained cadence. Target roles and team structures that supply that (a strong ops partner, clear strategic remit) rather than roles that are pure relentless operational grind with no support.

## Part D — The AI-era technical-leader layer

What makes a 2026 CTO/Head-of-Eng candidate credible (at my altitude — not IC ML):
- **Agentic architecture fluency** — identity, orchestration, and guardrails for agents (a direct extension of my IAM/security work). Best demonstrated by having *built* a small agentic system, not by a certificate.
- **AI governance literacy** — EU AI Act / NIST AI RMF / ISO 42001. Especially load-bearing for medtech and energy/regulated buyers. (IAGP-type credential is a strong, rare differentiator here.)
- **AI economics** — eval pipelines, cost-per-token, reliability trade-offs as first-class engineering concerns.
- **A visible point of view** — one reference architecture or talk ("agentic AI in regulated environments") signals strategy-layer thinking better than three certs.
